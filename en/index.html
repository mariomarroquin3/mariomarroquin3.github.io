Tail risk for Bitcoin and Ethereum

Which models best predict the tail risk of Bitcoin and Ethereum, and which ones fail?

Out-of-sample comparison of more than 20 volatility models that predict Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall one day ahead with a walk-forward protocol on a rolling 500-day window.

  • Models. GARCH-t, GJR, EGARCH, EWMA, FHS, GARCH-EVT, HAR-RV, Realized-GARCH, CAViaR, MS-GARCH, RF-QR, LSTM-Vol…
  • Evaluation. Kupiec, Christoffersen, Dynamic Quantile, Acerbi-Szekely, FZO, Diebold-Mariano, MCS and Berkowitz.
  • Finding. Realized-GARCH and CAViaR lead the FZO ranking; MS-GARCH fails to identify regimes with a 500-day window.
Summary

Out-of-sample comparison of more than 20 volatility and tail-risk models (GARCH family, HAR, CAViaR, MS-GARCH, RF-QR and LSTM) for BTC and ETH. Each model predicts one-day VaR and Expected Shortfall on a rolling 500-day window and is evaluated with coverage tests (Kupiec, Christoffersen), loss-based scores (FZO, Acerbi-Szekely) and comparison tests (Diebold-Mariano, MCS). Realized-GARCH and CAViaR lead the ranking; MS-GARCH fails to identify stable regimes with that window.

Entropy Arena

How unpredictable is a source that only looks random?

Compares randomness sources for generating cryptographic keys (BIP39/32/48/67, 2-of-3 and 3-of-5 multisig), separating statistical quality (NIST SP 800-22) from real entropy (min-entropy, NIST SP 800-90B).

  • Sources. CSPRNG, Mersenne Twister, RANDU, logistic map, Lorenz attractor, Rule 30, digits of π, CPU jitter, dice and card shuffles.
  • Attacks. State recovery of MT19937 in 0.02 s and of RANDU in 0.26 s, plus π-offset and clock-seed attacks.
  • Pipeline. BIP39 → BIP32 → BIP48 → BIP67, bech32 addresses (BIP173) and descriptors (BIP380).
Summary

Looking random is not the same as being unpredictable. The framework separates a source's statistical quality (NIST SP 800-22 tests) from its real entropy (min-entropy, NIST SP 800-90B) and measures how well an attacker can predict it. It recovers the MT19937 state in 0.02 s and the RANDU state in 0.26 s, and follows the impact all the way to BIP39/32/48 keys and multisig.

RetinoVisionAI

Can a neural network help detect diabetic retinopathy where ophthalmologists are scarce?

Classification of diabetic retinopathy in fundus images with convolutional neural networks. A computer-aided diagnosis tool designed for communities with limited access to ophthalmologists.

  • Model. CNN in PyTorch (EfficientNet-B3) with data augmentation, illumination normalization and stratified cross-validation.
  • Interpretability. Web app with Grad-CAM to see which regions of the retina drive each prediction.
  • Challenges. Noisy medical images, class imbalance and variability between patients.
Summary

A CNN (EfficientNet-B3, PyTorch) classifies diabetic retinopathy in fundus images. It is trained with data augmentation, illumination normalization and stratified cross-validation to handle noisy images, imbalanced classes and variability between patients. A web app with Grad-CAM shows which regions of the retina drive each prediction.

Financial data synthesizer with a VAE

Can accounting systems be tested and audited without exposing real data?

A Variational Autoencoder in PyTorch learns the statistical patterns of real financial transactions and generates new records, designed to test accounting software and audits without exposing customer data.

It includes the preprocessing pipeline (log transforms and one-hot encoding) and a Streamlit app where you upload a dataset, control how many records are generated, validate the resemblance with PCA and export the CSV.

Improving: measuring re-identification risk and comparing data fidelity with more complete metrics.

Summary

A VAE learns the joint distribution of financial transactions (amounts with a log transform and categorical variables with one-hot encoding) and samples its latent space to generate new records. A Streamlit app lets you upload a dataset, choose the volume, validate the resemblance with PCA and export the CSV. Re-identification risk still needs to be measured formally.

Security and economic growth in Central America

Does violence affect growth through institutions and investment?

Studies whether violence affects growth through institutional quality and foreign direct investment in Central America, Colombia and the Dominican Republic (2000–2024). A reproducible fixed-effects pipeline with inference robust to few clusters, plus an ML layer for triangulation.

Summary

Analyzes whether violence (homicides) deteriorates institutional quality (WGI) and, through that channel, foreign investment and growth, in 8 countries of Central America, Colombia and the Dominican Republic between 2000 and 2024. It uses two-way fixed effects with few-cluster inference (wild cluster bootstrap, CR2, Driscoll-Kraay), diagnostics and ML as triangulation. It is still under methodological review and makes no causal claim.
